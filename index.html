CNB Competency: Selects and evaluates appropriate descriptive research designs to investigate human behavior while accounting for ethical boundaries and methodological bias.
Learning Outcome: Matches specific psychological research questions with optimal descriptive methods (observation, survey, case study, correlation) and identifies potential biases.

Stage I: Research Question Warm-up & Method Direct Instruction

Duration: ~5-10 min

5-Min Warm-up Flash Review

Review this research question with your seat partner:

"Does daily social media usage exceed 3 hours correlate with higher self-reported anxiety scores in 10th graders?"
  1. Identify the Independent/Predictor Variable.
  2. Identify the Dependent/Outcome Variable.
  3. How could we gather data without manipulating their behavior in a lab?

5 Core Descriptive Research Methods

1. Naturalistic Observation: Observing behavior in real settings without intervention.
2. Surveys / Questionnaires: Self-reported data from large sample populations.
3. In-depth Interviews: Qualitative, structured or semi-structured personal dialogues.
4. Case Studies: Deep analysis of a single unique individual or rare phenomenon.
5. Correlational Research: Measuring statistical relationships between two existing variables.

Stage II: Method-Matching Activity & Bias Identification

Duration: 20 min

Read each research objective below. Select the best Descriptive Method, state its main strength, and identify a potential bias or limitation. Click to verify your answers!

Scenario 1

"Investigating the rare cognitive recovery pattern of a patient who survived a specific localized brain injury."

Best Method: Case Study

Key Strength: Provides rich, detailed, in-depth qualitative data on rare conditions impossible to replicate ethically.

Limitation / Bias: Low generalizability (findings cannot easily be applied to the broader population); potential researcher subjectivity.

Scenario 2

"Measuring cafeteria seating patterns and peer grouping choices among high school freshmen during lunch breaks without interacting with them."

Best Method: Naturalistic Observation

Key Strength: High ecological validity; captures authentic, natural behavior without laboratory pressure.

Limitation / Bias: Observer Bias (researcher sees what they expect); lack of experimental control over unexpected external events.

Scenario 3

"Gathering sleep duration and stress level ratings from 300 high school seniors across 3 campus branches in one week."

Best Method: Survey / Questionnaire (Correlational Design)

Key Strength: Efficient collection of large amounts of data quickly and cost-effectively.

Limitation / Bias: Social Desirability Bias (participants underreport bad habits); wording bias in questions.

Stage III: Project 1 Checkpoint & Team Workshop

Duration: 30 min
Research Director Ensures alignment between research question and chosen method.
Ethics & Bias Officer Identifies sample biases, observer effects, and ethical protocols.
Spokesperson Presents the project methodology proposal in the plenary.

Project 1 Checkpoint Task: Draft Methodological Blueprint

In your assigned teams, take your approved research question from Plan #2 and fill out the following 4 project checkpoint components:

1. Method Selection Choose between Observation, Survey, Interview, or Case Study. Justify why.
2. Target Sample Define sample size, demographic, and sampling procedure (e.g., random vs. convenience).
3. Bias Prevention Identify 1 primary source of bias (e.g., wording, observer) and how you will minimize it.
4. Data Collection Tool Draft 3 sample survey items or observation checklist criteria.

Stage IV: Plenary Presentations & Interactive Evaluation

Duration: 15 min

Plenary Share (10 min)

Each team spokesperson presents their Methodological Blueprint in 90 seconds. Peer review focused on bias control.

Exit Ticket (5 min)

On your exit index card: "Why does correlation NOT equal causation? Give one 1-sentence example."

Plenary Method Blueprint Rubric Interactive Scoring
Criteria Developing (1 pt) Proficient (2 pts) Exemplary (3 pts) Score
1. Method Appropriateness Selected method mismatch with research question. Method aligns well with research question. Method selection is flawlessly justified with clear methodological rationale.
2. Bias Identification Fails to identify methodological limitations. Identifies at least 1 relevant bias source. Accurately analyzes bias sources and proposes realistic controls.
3. Instrument Design Instrument items are vague or biased. Instrument items are mostly structured and clear. Drafted tools/items are precise, unbiased, and ready for pilot testing.
4. Team Collaboration & Synergy Roles were undefined or uncoordinated. Clear role division and active participation. High synergy, equal contribution, and polished presentation.
